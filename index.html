Part 1 • Core Grammar

Present Simple vs. Present Continuous

Compare how we form and use both present tenses to talk about routines, general facts, current actions, and temporary situations.

Present Simple

Permanent & Repeated

Form

  • + I / You / We / They work.  |  He / She / It works.
  • − I don’t work.  |  She doesn’t work.
  • ? Do you work?  |  Does he work?

When to Use

1. Habits, routines & repeated actions

“I usually drink black coffee before work.”

2. Permanent situations & general facts

“My sister lives in Lviv and teaches biology.”

3. Timetables & fixed schedules

“The last train leaves at 11:15 PM.”

Signal words: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never, every day, on Mondays, twice a week.

Present Continuous

In Progress & Temporary

Form (am / is / are + verb-ing)

  • + I am working.  |  He is working.  |  They are working.
  • − I’m not working.  |  She isn’t working.
  • ? Are you working?  |  Is he working?

When to Use

1. Actions happening right now

“Please be quiet — the baby is sleeping.”

2. Temporary situations around now

“I’m staying with my cousin while my flat is painted.”

3. Changing trends & future arrangements

“More people are working remotely these days.”

Signal words: now, right now, at the moment, currently, today, this week, these days, Look! Listen!

Part 2 • Crucial Rule

Active vs. Stative Verbs

!

Important Rule: Stative Verbs Are NOT Used in Continuous Tenses

Active (dynamic) verbs describe physical actions that we can start and stop (e.g., eat, run, cook, talk). They can be used in both Simple and Continuous tenses.
Stative verbs describe states, feelings, thoughts, senses, or possession rather than actions. Even when they are true right now, we use the Present Simple — never -ing.

Correct (Active & Stative)
  • ✓ “I am eating lunch right now.” (action)
  • ✓ “I know the answer to your question.” (state)
  • ✓ “She wants a glass of sparkling water.” (state)
  • ✓ “This soup smells delicious!” (state)
Incorrect (Avoid -ing)
  • ✗ “I am knowing the answer to your question.”
  • ✗ “She is wanting a glass of sparkling water.”
  • ✗ “This soup is smelling delicious!”
  • ✗ “Are you understanding what I mean?”

Common Stative Verbs by Category

Thoughts & Opinions

know, understand, believe, remember, forget, mean, agree, disagree, suppose, recognize, realize

Feelings & Desires

like, love, hate, prefer, want, need, wish, care, appreciate, mind

Senses & Perception

see, hear, smell, taste, sound, seem, appear, look (= seem)

Possession & Being

have (= own), own, belong, contain, include, consist of, cost, weigh, depend on, matter

B1 Dual-Meaning Tip (State vs. Action):

• think: “I think it’s great.” (opinion) vs. “I’m thinking about dessert.” (considering)

• have: “We have a reservation.” (possession) vs. “We’re having dinner.” (eating)

• taste: “The pasta tastes rich.” (quality) vs. “The chef is tasting the sauce.” (action)

• see: “I see what you mean.” (understand) vs. “I’m seeing the doctor today.” (meeting)
